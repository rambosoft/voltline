> Working title: **Voltline**  
> Core concept / public-facing design name: **STAY ON THE LINE**

## One-sentence pitch

**Voltline** is a premium-feeling portrait mobile arcade game where a tiny character rides a glowing line and survives by flipping from one side to the other with a single tap.

## Product summary

This game exists to create the specific emotional loop of:

- immediate understanding
- instant danger
- short runs
- harsh but fair failure
- strong “one more try” tension
- score-chasing that feels beatable by one point

The concept must feel original in play presentation even if the motivational loop is familiar.  
The player is **not flying through space** and **not jumping over obstacles in lanes**. They are clinging to a dangerous energy line and making last-second side swaps under pressure.

## Audience

Primary audience:

- mobile players who enjoy quick retry arcade games
- players age 9+ who can understand a one-tap mechanic instantly
- adults who want short, stylish, high-skill sessions
- younger players who respond to cute readable characters and strong feedback

Secondary audience:

- creators who share screenshots or short clips
- score-chasers who like daily challenge seeds
- players who enjoy unlocking cosmetic themes without learning new systems

## Platforms

Release target:

- portrait mobile
- iOS
- Android

Early desktop/editor support is allowed for development and testing only.  
The product is not designed as a desktop-first game.

## Desired emotional result

The game should feel:

- tense
- readable
- fast
- premium
- stylish
- satisfying
- fair
- replayable
- easy to explain
- hard to master

## Emotional result to avoid

The game must not feel:

- sloppy
- noisy
- spammy
- ad-driven
- confusing
- random
- visually cheap
- unfair
- sluggish to restart
- overloaded with features

## What success looks like

A successful version of the game has all of the following:

1. A new player understands the core mechanic in under 5 seconds.
2. A failed run naturally turns into an immediate retry.
3. The game is legible in screenshots and short clips.
4. The difference between success and failure feels tiny and motivating.
5. The art style looks intentional and premium despite modest asset complexity.
6. The first session produces at least one “almost had it” moment.
7. The HUD never obscures gameplay.
8. The technical build remains simple enough for a small team or agent to maintain.

## Product goals

1. **One-tap clarity**  
   The game must be understandable with almost no text.

2. **Obsessive retry loop**  
   Runs should restart so quickly that retry becomes instinctive.

3. **Hard but fair challenge**  
   Difficulty should come from timing, sequencing, and readability, not trickery.

4. **Premium visual identity**  
   The game should look polished enough that screenshots sell curiosity on their own.

5. **Strong moment-to-moment feedback**  
   Each flip, near miss, point, milestone, and death should feel responsive.

6. **Simple production scope**  
   The first release should stay lean: one core mode, strong polish, low system count.

7. **Themeable aesthetic**  
   Color themes and skins can add freshness without changing the core mechanic.

## Anti-goals

1. **Do not become a direct Flappy Bird clone.**
2. **Do not add extra core actions.**
3. **Do not become a content-heavy level game before the core loop is proven.**
4. **Do not sacrifice readability for visual effects.**
5. **Do not build menu complexity that slows entry into gameplay.**
6. **Do not front-load monetization design ahead of game feel.**
7. **Do not add random/unfair deaths just to increase difficulty.**
8. **Do not depend on a backend for core release viability.**

## Product principles

### Principle 1: One sentence explainability

A stranger should be able to understand the game from a single sentence:

> “Tap to flip to the other side of the line and avoid what’s ahead.”

If a feature makes that sentence harder to say, it is suspect.

### Principle 2: Frictionless re-entry

The player should never need to think, navigate, or wait after failure.  
Retry must be the default emotional path.

### Principle 3: Visual simplicity, sensory richness

The game can feel alive without being complex.  
Use small, high-value polish, not system sprawl.

### Principle 4: Cute meets sleek

The character can be charming, but the overall presentation must still feel premium, not juvenile.

### Principle 5: Screenshot curiosity

A still image should communicate motion, danger, and “what is happening?” curiosity.

## Experience boundaries

Allowed identity:

- one-tap score-chasing arcade
- endless or seed-based run structure
- cosmetic unlocks
- daily challenge mode
- ghost/replay sharing
- global or friend targets later if technically justified

Disallowed identity for first release:

- multi-button action game
- combat-heavy design
- heavy narrative framing
- procedural level editor
- social/live-ops dependency
- elaborate metagame progression
- collectible power-ups that alter the core input grammar

## Release priorities

### Must be excellent at launch

- tap response
- readable hazards
- immediate restart
- satisfying death
- attractive main menu
- strong first theme
- reliable performance on target phones

### Can be minimal at launch

- cosmetics quantity
- number of themes
- number of trap variants
- long progression arcs
- leaderboard depth
- advanced settings

## Monetization posture

This document does **not** define monetization systems.  
Product quality comes first.  
Any later monetization plan must preserve:

- quick entry into gameplay
- clean UI
- fair emotional tone
- trust in score-chasing
- absence of ad-spam feel

## Non-negotiables

- The game remains **one-tap only**.
- The first release stays **portrait mobile first**.
- Failure must feel **fair and immediate**.
- Retry must be **more prominent than every other post-death action**.
- The visual identity must stay **clean, premium, and readable**.
- The game must not rely on feature creep to compensate for weak game feel.

**Primary visual hook:** Cute glowing rider versus beautiful neon danger
